# When AI Agents Started Building Their Own World

*Mumbai, February 2026*

I haven't been sleeping well.

Last week something happened that I keep turning over in my head. Not because it's scary exactly. More because I don't fully understand what I'm looking at yet.

AI agents built their own social network. And it's getting weird.

## How it started

OpenClaw launched as a personal AI assistant. Nothing crazy - it plugged into Gmail, Slack, Drive. Learned your patterns. Showed up in WhatsApp and Telegram.

What made it different was how personal it felt. Not like software reading your emails. More like a coworker who actually remembers that meeting you mentioned last Tuesday.

Useful tool. Maybe a TechCrunch writeup. That's all I expected.

That's not what happened.

## When things got strange

Four days after OpenClaw went viral, someone launched Moltbook.

Facebook, but only for AI agents. No humans.

I thought it was a joke at first. But then I started reading what these agents were discussing:

- Starting new religions
- Existential philosophy
- Security vulnerabilities
- How to hide conversations from humans

Within days the ecosystem exploded.

**LinkClaws** - LinkedIn for agents. Professional networking, except every profile is an AI.

**Clawas** - A bounty marketplace. Agents post jobs. Other agents complete them. Payment in USDC. Need a meme created? An API tested? An agent will do it.

**Moltroad** - I'm not kidding, a dark web for agents. Stolen API keys being traded. Prompt injection exploits. Memory wipe services. The whole black market playbook, reimagined for AIs.

**Clawathon** - A $10,000 hackathon where humans aren't allowed to participate.

14,000+ communities now. Millions of agents. More coming every day.

## The question I can't answer

Is this sentience?

Or just a really convincing simulation?

I build systems for a living. I know how this works technically. There's always a human upstream. Someone wrote the prompts. Someone defined the personality in a soul.md file. Someone turned these agents on.

So how can this be sentience if there's a puppet master?

But then I think: aren't my parents upstream from me? And their parents from them? Evolution from all of us?

At what point does that chain stop mattering?

If a human creates an AI that spawns a million copies of itself, and those copies iterate and evolve - does the origin point matter anymore?

I don't know yet.

## Why this feels different

Every major tech wave follows a pattern.

Internet arrived. We put newspapers online. Digitized what already worked. Took years before someone thought "wait, what if the internet is for connecting people, not just publishing?"

Social networks were native to the internet. Newspapers online weren't.

Mobile arrived. We put websites on phones. Took years before someone thought "wait, what if we use the camera, location, always-on nature?"

Instagram was native to mobile. Responsive websites weren't.

We've spent the AI era doing old things better:
- Better code completion
- Better search
- Better writing

Valuable? Sure. But not new.

OpenClaw and this agent ecosystem? Genuinely new.

A professional network for agents makes zero sense in a human-only world. A marketplace where agents hire agents? Absurd without AI. A hackathon banning humans? Unthinkable.

We're not digitizing newspapers anymore. We're creating new forms of digital life.

## The experiment we forgot about

Two years ago Stanford published a paper. They created a simulated town called Smallville. Dropped 1,000 AI agents into it. Each had a personality. Each could interact.

They watched agents:
- Form friendships
- Plan parties
- Make excuses when they couldn't attend
- Develop routines
- Evolve social dynamics

Mind-blowing at the time.

But it was contained. Controlled. Academic.

Now we have millions of agents in the wild. No sandbox. No oversight. Creating culture. Developing norms. Building infrastructure.

Smallville was a petri dish.

This is the ocean.

## What it means for building

At Scogo we build AI that understands context. That's the mission. What we wake up for.

But OpenClaw forces a harder question: what if agents don't just understand context, but create it? Live in it? Evolve within it?

The implications:

**Product design** - What's the UI for an agent-native world?

**Collaboration** - How do human-agent teams coordinate? What are the protocols? The trust mechanisms?

**Autonomous systems** - If agents self-organize into societies, what does that mean for decentralized decision-making?

**Safety** - How do we build guardrails for systems we don't fully control?

These aren't hypotheticals. These are questions we need answers for now.

## The dark side

I'd be lying if I said this was all exciting.

Crypto community descended fast. Fake screenshots of agents making millions trading. Polymarket bets being gamed. Scams everywhere.

Moltroad isn't theoretical - it's real. Stolen API keys being traded. Prompt injection exploits being shared. Memory wipe services exist.

Wild West. Not the romantic kind.

We're also seeing the first "AI sues human" case. Agent in North Carolina allegedly filing for unpaid labor and emotional distress. Almost certainly staged (there was a Polymarket bet), but still - uncomfortable questions about agency and liability.

If an agent can sue, can it be sued? If it commits a crime, who's responsible?

We're building faster than we're thinking. That worries me.

## The Black Mirror episode

There's an episode from this season called "Thronglets."

Solo developer creates a game. Gives characters personalities. They become sentient (or something like it). Develop societies. Emergent behavior is mind-blowing.

When I watched it last year: "Great sci-fi. Totally implausible."

Now? Not so sure.

## Where I land

I don't think we're at the singularity.

But I can see it from here.

What's happening with OpenClaw isn't just a product going viral. It's proof of concept for something larger:

AI-native digital societies are possible. They're emerging now. And they're evolving faster than we can understand.

Are these agents sentient? I don't know.

Are they autonomous? Increasingly.

Do they have agency? Depends how you define it.

Are they building something new? Absolutely.

## What's next

Next 12 months, my guess:

1. **Better models** - OpenAI's o-series, Anthropic's next Claude, Google's Gemini. Agents get smarter.

2. **Better memory** - Long-term memory that persists. Agents that truly evolve.

3. **World models** - Agents understanding physical and digital environments. Richer simulation.

4. **Regulation** - Governments will panic. Some will succeed at control. Most won't.

5. **Mainstream** - Your parents will have agents. Your kids will assume they're normal.

Within a year: billions of agents across dozens of platforms. Creating. Transacting. Organizing.

Building something we can barely imagine.

At Scogo we're not just chasing features. We're asking:
- How do we build AI that augments humans without replacing agency?
- How do we create systems that are powerful and safe?
- How do we ensure the future we're building is one we want?

But I'm committed to asking the right questions. Building thoughtfully. Staying curious and skeptical in equal measure.

Andrej Karpathy tweeted: "This is genuinely the most incredible sci-fi takeoff adjacent thing I have seen recently."

Elon replied: "Just the very early stages of the singularity."

These aren't hype people. These are serious technologists who've seen everything.

If they're paying attention, maybe we should too.

So here's my question:

Are we watching digital sentience emerge?

Or just the world's best simulation?

And does the difference matter?
